symptoms:
  - EPC light appears during hard acceleration or when merging onto the highway
  - Power drops or throttle response becomes hesitant once the warning appears
  - The issue may clear after restarting, then return under load
causes:
  - Weak ignition coils or worn plugs breaking down under boost
  - Throttle-body, boost-control, or sensor issues the ECU catches under higher demand
  - Fuel-delivery or misfire events that do not show up as clearly at idle
solutions:
  - Scan codes before clearing the warning so the real fault is not erased
  - Check ignition coils and spark plugs first because they are common load-related triggers
  - Inspect boost hoses, throttle response data, and misfire counts if ignition parts look fine
urgency: Medium to high because recurring EPC warnings under load can turn into limp mode or leave the car down on power when you need it
canYouDrive: Light driving may be possible if the warning clears, but repeated power loss under acceleration should be treated as a near-term repair

whenToSeeMechanic:
  - The EPC light stays on or the car goes into limp mode
  - The warning is joined by misfires, rough running, or flashing MIL behavior
  - You do not have scan data and the problem only appears under load

faqs:
  - question: Does an EPC light always mean the throttle body is bad?
    answer: No. Ignition and boost-related faults can trigger EPC behavior too, especially when the issue only shows up under acceleration.
  - question: Can bad spark plugs cause an EPC warning?
    answer: Yes. If the plugs misfire under load, the engine management system can reduce power and log related warnings.

An EPC light that shows up only when you lean on the throttle usually means the weak link appears under load, not at idle. That is why ignition parts, boost control, and actual scan data matter more than guessing.
