symptoms:
  - Low humming or growling that increases with road speed
  - Noise changes slightly when steering weight shifts left or right
  - Vibration or roughness felt through the floor at highway speed
causes:
  - Worn wheel bearing or hub assembly
  - Tire cupping or uneven tread wear
  - Driveline or differential noise in more advanced cases
solutions:
  - Rotate tires and road-test again before replacing a hub blindly
  - Inspect hub play and listen for bearing roughness with the wheel off the ground
  - Replace the noisy hub assembly and verify alignment or tire condition afterward
urgency: Medium because the noise itself may start small, but a failing bearing should not be ignored for long
canYouDrive: Sometimes for a short period, but only if the noise is mild and there is no looseness, vibration spike, or heat from the hub area
estimatedCost: $0 to $600 depending on whether the issue is only tire noise, a hub assembly, or added alignment and tire work
diyDifficulty: Easy for basic tire checks, moderate to hard for bearing confirmation and hub replacement
whenToSeeMechanic:
  - The noise is getting louder week by week
  - The sound changes clearly with steering load or is joined by vibration
  - There is wheel play, heat, or roughness from the hub area
commonMistakes:
  - Replacing a wheel bearing before doing a tire rotation test
  - Assuming every highway hum is a bad hub
  - Ignoring alignment and tire wear after the noisy part is replaced
quickVerdict: A speed-related hum should be treated like a tire-versus-bearing question first, not an excuse to buy a hub immediately.
firstCheck: Rotate the tires and road-test again before committing to a wheel-bearing diagnosis.
confusedWith:
  - Tire cupping gets blamed on hubs constantly because the cabin noise sounds more dramatic than the tread wear looks.
  - Owners also confuse minor road-surface noise with bearing failure before load changes or rotation tests tell a clearer story.
stopDrivingIf:
  - The hum turns into roughness, heat, wheel play, or a stronger vibration through the floor and steering.
  - You can feel looseness or hear the noise changing aggressively with steering load.

faqs:
  - question: How do I know if the noise is a tire or a wheel bearing?
    answer: Tire noise often changes after rotation, while bearing noise usually follows load shifts and keeps getting louder as wear progresses.
  - question: Is a humming wheel bearing a safety issue?
    answer: It can become one. Early noise may seem minor, but a failing bearing should be replaced before play or heat buildup gets worse.

This is one of the most misdiagnosed noise complaints because tires and bearings can sound similar from inside the cabin. A quick rotation test and a careful road test can save you from replacing the wrong part.
